## CHAPTER 8 – GIVING A NO
- But I was learning a tremendous amount about **psychology, negotiation, and persuasion**.
	- Learning all this by getting rejected and analysing what and how it went wrong. The same thing is the secret behind success in Chess and attempting competitive examinations. Analysing my own mistakes has been one of my weakest points since childhood – the fear is too much. ^3c5a71
- Being the person doing the rejection didn’t feel much better to me than being on its receiving end.
- I realized that if I didn’t get a handle on my reluctance to reject people, then I might end up wanting to quit as well.
- Saying yes to requests, respononsibilities all the time builds a resentment for not getting anything in return for doing more. The frustration starts to build and it results in quitting – either physically or mentally, like at 			- [[My Experiences While Transitioning Across Companies#Kreatryx|Kreatryx]].
- I was afraid they would reject me because of my rejection.
- Not all rejections are created equal. There are **good rejections and bad rejections**. Some of the people who had rejected me had been **harsh and dismissive**, but others had said no with such **finesse and kindness** that they managed to make me like them despite their nos.

#### 100 DAYS OF REJECTION: EXCHANGE SERVICES WITH A PERSONAL TRAINER
- My goal was to **get a personal trainer to give me an hour of free training without me joining his gym**. In exchange, I would offer to spend an hour teaching him everything I’d learned about entrepreneurship and blogging.
- He [Jordan] wasn’t dismissive, even though my request held no interest for him. He heard me out, and showed that he was taking me seriously by giving me real reasons why the request wasn’t going to work. **He made me feel valued by pulling out his problem-solving skills and doing what he could to help me get what I wanted.**
- Jordan had delivered a **“perfect rejection.”**

#### PATIENCE AND RESPECT
- **Being patient and respectful when saying no is such a simple concept.**
- Sometimes a good interaction and a bad one are separated by nothing but a smile.
- Showing **patience and respect can soften the blow of rejection**, and sometimes even earn the other person’s respect and understanding.
- **Moral of the story: If you have to reject someone, do it nicely.**

#### BE DIRECT
- A lot of people have the tendency to give indirect, sugarcoated rejections. They [indirect, sugarcoated rejections] usually come in two forms: **big setups and yes-buts**.
- With **“big setups,”** rejectors spend a long time explaining the reason for their rejection before they actually deliver it. Companies are notoriously good at this.
	- Eg, rejection letters during the hiring process, loan rejection etc.
- **“Yes-buts”** happen when the rejector verbally acknowledges or even validates a request, then uses the word but or unfortunately to deliver the rejection.
- Eg. requests declined by customer care etc.
- [[Conversation Transformation]] by [[Ben E. Benjamin]]
- **The danger of “yes-buts.”** – not only do they send mixed messages, but also they make the idea difficult for the rejectee’s brain to process and could elicit a defensive response.
- **When you deliver a rejection to someone, give the bad news quickly and directly.**

#### OFFER ALTERNATIVES
- 100 DAYS OF REJECTION: SPEAKING OVER COSTCO’S INTERCOM
- **A great way to reject someone: by offering an alternative.**
- The person rejecting me was making it clear that he was **rejecting my request—not rejecting me as a person.**
- Most people can’t separate the rejection from who they are as a person. It takes practice and conscious thinking to separate the two and not take things personally.
