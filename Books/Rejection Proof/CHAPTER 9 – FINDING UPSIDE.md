## CHAPTER 9 – FINDING UPSIDE
> **“That which does not kill us makes us stronger.”**
– [[Friedrich Nietzsche]]

#### MOTIVATION
- One of the biggest upsides of rejection is that it can serve as motivation.
- The relationship you have with a rejection can be negative or positive, and it all depends on which way you spin it for yourself.
- [[Michael Jordan]]’s **2009 Basketball Hall of Fame** induction speech was anything but boring. He consistently used rejection as motivation during his career and even into retirement.
	- His attitude of converting opinions of his critics, detractors and rivals as a fuel to fire his own performance. It also led to the birth of this meme from the Netflix documentary [[The Last Dance]].
		![[Pasted image 20201003201942.png]]

**Successful people convert rejection into personal fuel.**
- Quarterback [[Tom Brady]] was passed over 198 times in the 2000 NFL draft before finally being selected by the New England Patriots. Brady has since become one of the greatest quarterbacks of all time, winning three Super Bowls and counting.
- [[Apple]] founder [[Steve Jobs]] was told by a playmate that he was unwanted and abandoned.
- [[Rejection Proof by Jia Jiang#REJECTION HAS A NUMBER|Some more examples of success before numerous rejections]]
- Each of them saw rejection as “wood on the fire,” as Michael Jordan so eloquently put it. It simply added more flame to the ambition they already had brewing.

#### WORTHINESS
- Countless examples of **people who were rejected or even persecuted for their beliefs but vindicated by time.**
- Galileo’s scientific theories being declared heretical.
- Vincent van Gogh, whose work now sells for millions but who was deemed a failure during his own lifetime,
- Companies, organizations, parents, teachers, and our society as a whole universally praise creativity and thinking outside of the box. However, **when creativity actually happens, it is often met with rejection, because it frequently disrupts order and rules.**
- The Innovator’s Dilemma, Clayton Christensen
- A study done by University of Pennsylvania psychologist Jennifer Mueller is called **The Bias Against Creativity: Why People Desire but Reject Creative Ideas.**
- Creativity presents a level of uncertainty. As human beings, we crave certain and predictable outcomes. 
- George Bernard Shaw famously said, **“All great truths begin as blasphemies.”**
> **“First they ignore you, then they laugh at you, then they fight you, then you win.”**
– Mahatma Gandhi
- If someone thinks your idea is “incredibly stupid,” consider the possibility that you might be onto something.

#### LESSONS
1. **Motivation:** Rejection can be used as one of the strongest motivations to fuel someone’s fire for achievement.
2. **Self-Improvement:** By taking the motion out of rejection, one can use it as an effective way to improve an idea or product.
3. **Worthiness:** Sometimes it is good to be rejected, especially if public opinion is heavily influenced by group and conventional thinking, and if the idea is radically creative.
4. **Character Building:** By seeking rejection in tough environments, one can build up the mental toughness to take on greater goals.
