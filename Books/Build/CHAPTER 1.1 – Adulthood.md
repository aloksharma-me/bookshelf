### CHAPTER 1.1 – Adulthood 

>[!AI Summary]
>Adulthood is often seen as the time when learning ends and living begins, but it's actually about continually making mistakes and learning from them. The best way to find a fulfilling career is to follow your natural curiosity and take risks, understanding that many of your early choices might fail. In your twenties, failure is part of the process—doing, failing, and learning are essential steps toward eventual success. It's important to have a goal and take steps toward it, even if you stumble along the way.
>
>At companies like General Magic, the idea of a joyful, playful workplace was central to creating innovative products. The process of learning through trial and error is critical, and the most important lesson is to keep moving forward. Over time, a broader understanding of business, including sales, marketing, and management, becomes as valuable as technical skills. Ultimately, it’s the combination of hard work, learning, and a willingness to fail that shapes success.

- Adulthood is commonly thought of as the time when learning is over and living begins. 
- **Adulthood is your opportunity to screw up continually until you learn how to screw up a little bit less.** 
- So when you’re looking at the array of potential careers before you, the correct place to start is this: “What do I want to learn?” 
- **The best way to find a job you’ll love and a career that will eventually make you successful is to follow what you’re naturally interested in, then take risks when choosing where to work.** Follow your curiosity rather than a business school playbook about how to make money. Assume that for much of your twenties your choices will not work out and the companies you join or start will likely fail. **Early adulthood is about watching your dreams go up in flames and learning as much as you can from the ashes.** Do, fail, learn. The rest will follow. 
- **ask for forgiveness, not permission.** 

> “The only failure in your twenties is inaction. The rest is trial and error.” —ANONYMOUS 

- **Do, fail, learn.** 
- The critical thing is to have a goal. To strive for something big and hard and important to you. Then every step you take toward that goal, even if it’s a stumble, moves you forward. 
- Humans learn through productive struggle, by trying it themselves and screwing up and doing it differently next time. 
- General Magic was probably one of the first Silicon Valley companies that truly embodied the idea that playing at work was worthwhile—that a joyful workplace could make a joyful product. 
- **You should never kill yourself for your job, and no job should ever expect that of you.** 
- Susan is a brilliant artist who created the original visual language for the Mac, and she made an entire beautiful world for the Magic Link. 
- When we finally shipped in 1994, we hadn’t made the Pocket Crystal. We made the Sony Magic Link. 
- By the time General Magic was unraveling around me, I wasn’t just a lowly diagnostics engineer anymore. I’d worked on silicon, hardware, and software architecture and design. When things started to go awry, I’d ventured out and started talking to people in sales and marketing, began learning about psychographics and branding, finally grasped the importance of managers, of process, of limits. After four years, I realised **there was a whole world of thinking that was needed before a line of code should be written**. And that thinking was fascinating. That thinking was what I wanted to do.
