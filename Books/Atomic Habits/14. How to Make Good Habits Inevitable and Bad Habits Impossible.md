### 14. How to Make Good Habits Inevitable and Bad Habits Impossible

> [!Summary]
> Applying the [[The Four Laws of Behaviour Change|3rd law of Behaviour Change]], reduce friction to near zero for good habits to occur effortlessly. In addition, increase friction to the point where it makes bad habits impossible to exist.
> 
> One way is to use a [[Commitment Device|commitment device]] which restricts bad habits from happening. Another is to automate good habits using [[One time actions to lock in good habits|one-time actions]] (eg. delete social media apps from phone) and technology.


---

- Sometimes success is less about making good habits easy and more about making bad habits hard. This is an inversion of the 3rd Law of Behavior Change: make it difficult. 
- make your bad habits more difficult by creating what psychologists call a commitment device. 
- A commitment device is a choice you make in the present that controls your actions in the future.2 It is a way to lock in future behavior, bind you to good habits, and restrict you from bad ones. 
- Commitment devices are useful because they enable you to take advantage of good intentions before you can fall victim to temptation. 
- The key is to change the task such that it requires more work to get out of the good habit than to get started on 
- Commitment devices increase the odds that you’ll do the right thing in the future by making bad habits difficult in the present.

#### How to Automate a Habit and Never Think About it Again
- The best way to break a bad habit is to make it impractical to do. Increase the friction until you don’t even have the option to act.

#### One Time Actions That Lock in Good Habits
- Mathematician and philosopher Alfred North Whitehead wrote, **“Civilisation advances by extending the number of operations we can perform without thinking about them.”**
- When working in your favour, automation can make your good habits inevitable and your bad habits impossible. It is the ultimate way to lock in future behaviour rather than relying on willpower in the moment. By utilising commitment devices, strategic onetime decisions, and technology, you can create an environment of inevitability—a space where good habits are not just an outcome you hope for but an outcome that is virtually guaranteed.
