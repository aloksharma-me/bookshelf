## A. Understand
The primary goal of Design Thinking is to have a solid understanding of the problem. In the Understand phase, we formulate the problem statement, broaden or narrow down the problem using various tools and techniques.

### 1. Problem Statement
- Problem Statement PDF Template
	- ![[Problem.pdf]]
- This is the starting point of any project/requirement and in Design Thinking process.
- This is key tool to consolidate the knowledge of the problems.
- Documenting this helps in understanding the correct problem to solve.
- To document the problem statement, we can ask the following questions to understand the problem, actors and the context
	- What is the problem? Why is it a problem?
	- Who has the problem? Who has a need?
	- When and where does the problem occur?
	- How is it being solved today?
	- Screenshot
		- ![[Pasted image 20240122103327.png]]
- To take it to the next step, we can extend the individual problem statements into an overarching problem in the form of "How might we redesign ... (what?) ... (for whom?) ... so that (their needs) ... are satisfied?"
- 'How might we' statements can be used as a shortcut to create use-cases to move into the solution phase.
- Outlining a clear problem statement is essential for targeted generation of ideas. A clearly defined problem statement helps setup a solid foundation for the successful development of creativity and creative problem solving.
- Over the duration of the project, the problem definition and solutions can influence and change each other. These should be updated as and when the changes happen.
- Note:
	- In Tekion, Subroto created a [design document template](https://www.notion.so/aloksharma/Design-Documentation-Template-dbc277b2c5234c54863c1eb5d67de2bd?pvs=4) to document all design projects. In the problem space, it included 3 questions
		- Why are we designing this?
		- Who are we designing the experience for?
		- How do we judge success?

### 2. Design Principles
- Design Principles PDF Template
	- ![[Design Principles.pdf]]
- These are the guidelines that creates a framework for the team to take decisions during the project/requirement
- It helps define general characteristics that should be treated with higher priority.
- The process of creating and finalising the design principles can go like this
	- Add all design principles in a 'basket' (or section in FigJam)
	- Organise them in a pyramid with multiple sections going from project specific principles on top, general principles at the bottom of the pyramid.
- A reference website for [Design Principle examples](https://www.designprinciplesftw.com/).
- Design Principles are a form of constraints added to limit the endless creative freedom. They help coming to a decision during the project.

### 3. Interview for Empathy
- Interview PDF Template
	- ![[Interview for Empathy.pdf]]
- Interviews helps understand the problems from user's point of view and build empathy with a user.
- What we can do with this tool
	- The interviews helps designers build an understanding of user's needs, emotions, motivations and ways of thinking.
	- Gain insights that would be hidden with superficial considerations (eg. user frustrations, deeper motives)
	- Find out context of the usage of the flows, their mental models etc
	- Validate information we already have
- How the tool is applied
	- ﻿﻿Introduce yourself, explain the problem you are trying to solve with the interview
	- Emphasise that the interview is not about finding a solution, rather learn about the user's problems, motivations.
	- Build a relationship with the user, make them comfortable to be able to share personal stories related to the problem.
	- Avoid interrupting them as much as possible.
	- User open questions (W+H questions) to understand their motives. Avoid yes/no questions.
	- Ask questions to help the user think from a different perspectives.
	- Pay attention to gestures, body language, facial expressions.
- Interview template
	- ![[Pasted image 20240122132328.png]]
- Create a question map (in the form of a mind-map) for an in-depth interview. Write W+H questions around the topic in the centre. Do not use structured questionnaire to avoid confirmation bias.
- User journey stages in addition to question map to gain new insights about their workflow.
- Interview tips (more on pages 60-61)
	- Take an interview partner who is well-versed to the problem (eg. PM, PSM etc)
	- Ask simple, closed-ended questions at the beginning, then ask more open-ended questions.
	- Use storytelling to share the insights with the design team.
	- Record the interview, take notes and go in pairs since it's difficult to remember everything.

### 5. Ask 5x Why
- PDF Template for 5 Whys
	- ![[Ask5x.pdf]]
- This is to ensure we understand the problem in depth and not just scratch the surface.
- Asking why helps to dig deeper to gain new and surprising insights, more than just obvious symptoms, and true cause of a problem.
- Asking why also unravels problems that user's won't mention if only asked once.
- How the tool is applied
	- Describe the problem in detail. Use photos/sketches to illustrate it.
	- Start with 'root cause analysis' and ask why as often as possible. Try to counter each answer with a follow-up question.
	- Move onto another problem once exhausted all whys.
	- Also use 'how' as another variation of this technique.
- 'Why' helps design teams to understand the problem, 'How' helps us to ideate on solving it.
- 5 Why template example
	- ![[Pasted image 20240122135419.png]]

### 6. 5W+H Questions
- PDF Template
	- ![[5 Wh Questions Basic.pdf]]
- This tool helps in gaining in-depth insights, find new information to understand the problem or situation holistically.
- 5W+H questions are simply questions beginning with W or H: who, what, when, where, why and how.
- Investigative journalists also use this tool in their research.
- During the observation phase, this tool helps to explore 'what' happens, 'where' it happens and 'how' it happens.
- This tool can be applied in many different situations. Eg.
	- to understand the problem better
	- to learn more about user needs
- Template example
	- ![[Pasted image 20240122172203.png]]
- Tips
	- 5W+H in combination with other interviewing techniques allow designer to gather as much information as possible.
	- Turning questions into negative can bring advantage and encourage creativity. Eg. when does the problem NOT occur?

### 7. Jobs to be Done (JTBD)
- JTBD PDF Template
	- ![[JobsToBeDone Basic EN.pdf]]
- This tool helps in focussing the solutions on things that can add value to the customer and help them complete their tasks.
- This tool can help in capturing customer tasks in a structured way and gain new insights.
- With this form of documenting user tasks, it allows designers to align to the job uses have to perform.
- The basic idea of JTBD is that users/customers 'scream' for a product or service whenever they have to solve a problem.
- How the tool is applied
	- It consists of 3 elements
		- description of the situation
		- explanation of the motivation
		- expected result
	- To understand tasks and goals, use other tools like 5W+H, 5x Why
	- Write down customer task in the form of "When... (situation), I want to... (motivation), so I can... (expected result)."

### 8. Extreme/Lead Users (Power Users)
- PDF Template
	- ![[Extreme User Lead User Basic.pdf]]
- Extreme/Lead users are also called power users/expert users.
- This tool helps to explore user needs that an average user is not able to articulate.
- It helps find innovative ideas and identify early trends in user needs and behaviours.
- It helps in ideating for [inclusive design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/inclusive-design/#:~:text=sense%20of%20belonging.-,Inclusive%20design%20describes%20methodologies%20to%20create%20products%20that%20understand%20and,location%2C%20language%2C%20and%20race.).
- Lead/power users have needs way ahead of the mass market. Often, these types of users already have a solution/workaround for their needs even if the solution is not supported by existing tools and features.
- Extreme users are the ones who uses a product/feature significantly more than an average user (eg. taxi driver vs average car driver)
- Designing for extreme users also helps designing for the wider market that may reach to the extreme level through training or gradually.

- Expert tips
	- Conversations with extreme uses often leads to wild and innovative ideas.
	- Staying in touch with extreme/power users via WhatsApp groups, blogs, Slack communities etc helps. (eg. Figma, Obsidian have such communities)
