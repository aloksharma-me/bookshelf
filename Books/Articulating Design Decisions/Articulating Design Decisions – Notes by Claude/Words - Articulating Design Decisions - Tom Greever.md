# Words — Articulating Design Decisions (Tom Greever)

Source: [[Articulating Design Decisions - Tom Greever]]

Notable terms, jargon, and coinages from the highlights, with brief definitions.

- **Bike-shedding** — spending disproportionate meeting time on a trivial, easily understood issue while ignoring the harder, more important ones. From Parkinson's Law of Triviality.

- **Painting a duck / the duck** — deliberately inserting an obvious, removable element into a design so stakeholders focus on cutting *it* instead of altering the parts you care about. (Attributed to Jeff Atwood.)

- **Hairy arms** — a manipulation tactic of intentionally making an unwanted option unappealing so the decision-maker rejects it in favor of your preferred one. (From 1940s Disney animation lore.)

- **CEO button** — Greever's coinage for an unexpected executive request to add a feature that destroys the balance of a project.

- **Home page syndrome** — Greever's coinage: the condition where the home screen becomes a dumping ground for every link, button, and banner, destroying usability.

- **Design-by-committee** — a pejorative for design driven by the merged, compromised suggestions of a group rather than a coherent vision.

- **Primacy and recency** — the tendency to best remember the first and last items in a sequence.

- **Reflective data** — data collected *after* a design change to compare before-and-after results (contrasted with "existing data" already on hand).

- **Popover / tooltip / drop list / input element / UI control** — interface-jargon examples Greever cites as terms stakeholders typically *don't* use.

- **Relational capital** — the influence and goodwill a person has built within an organization, which can be leveraged to support a decision.

- **KPIs (key performance indicators)** — the metrics a business tracks to gauge success.

- **Cognitive load** — the mental effort required to process information; Greever argues good preparation and note-taking reduce it.

- **Fidelity (low/high)** — the level of detail and polish in a design artifact, from rough wireframes (low) to ship-ready visuals (high).

- **Prototyping FTW** — heading using the internet shorthand "FTW" ("for the win"), endorsing prototypes for conveying user flows.

- **Pregame / huddle** — informal terms for the quick pre-meeting alignment with your support network.
