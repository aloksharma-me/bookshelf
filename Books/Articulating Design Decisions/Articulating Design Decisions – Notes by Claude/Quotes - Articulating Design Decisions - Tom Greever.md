# Quotes — Articulating Design Decisions (Tom Greever)

Source: [[Articulating Design Decisions - Tom Greever]]

Direct quotations that appear within the highlights, attributed to their source.

- "When someone offers you a challenge, don't think of all the reasons why you can't do it. Instead, say, 'Yes!' Then figure out how you'll get it done." — **Tom Greever** (author), describing the foundational principle of leading with a yes.

- "Leading with a yes gives you time to figure out if you can really pull it off... We're not making the case for having no boundaries; we're describing how innovation happens." / "And innovation happens in places where 'No' is seldom heard..." — drawn from the improv philosophy popularized by **Tina Fey** in *Bossypants* (the "Yes, and" rule of improvisation that Greever builds on). *Inferred source — the highlight itself does not name the speaker; flagged for your confirmation.*

- "a feature added for no other reason than to draw management attention and be removed, thus avoiding unnecessary changes in other aspects of the product." — **Jeff Atwood** (software developer, co-founder of Stack Overflow; writer of the *Coding Horror* blog), defining a "duck."

- "Eventually, it came time for the producer to review the animation set for the queen.... When the animations were done, he turned to the artist and said, 'That looks great. Just one thing: get rid of the duck.'" — **Coding Horror** (Jeff Atwood's blog), recounting the Interplay Entertainment chess-game story.

- "Final: drop-down control should be a pop-over menu." — **Tom Greever** (author), example of a definitive note.

- "What I hear you saying..." — **Tom Greever** (author), recommended phrase for repeating back stakeholder feedback.

- "Thank, Repeat, Prepare." — **Tom Greever** (author), his named approach for making a transition before responding.
