# Bibliography — Articulating Design Decisions (Tom Greever)

Source: [[Articulating Design Decisions - Tom Greever]]

People, works, companies, and concepts referenced within the highlights.

## Concepts & Frameworks

- *Parkinson's Law of Triviality* (a.k.a. "bike-shedding") — the principle that people in a meeting spend a disproportionate amount of time on issues that are not central to the project. Coined by **C. Northcote Parkinson**. Cited by Greever to explain why stakeholders fixate on trivial details.

- *Painting a duck / "the duck"* — a feature deliberately added so it can be removed, diverting management's attention away from changes you actually want to keep. Defined via **Jeff Atwood**.

- *Hairy arms* — an analogous tactic from 1940s Disney animation: animators added arm hair to characters they did not want approved, exploiting art directors' unconscious bias so their preferred (clean) designs would be chosen instead.

- *Primacy and recency* — memory principle that people best remember the first and last things said; Greever applies it to structuring meetings.

- *IDEAL response* — Greever's framework: Identify the problem, Describe your solution, Empathize with the user, Appeal to the business, Lock in agreement.

- *The Big Three questions* — What problem does it solve? How does it affect the user? Why is it better than the alternative?

- *"Thank, Repeat, Prepare"* — Greever's transition technique.

## People & Companies

- **Jeff Atwood** — software developer, co-founder of Stack Overflow / Stack Exchange, author of the *Coding Horror* blog. Source of the "duck" definition and the Interplay story.

- **Coding Horror** — Jeff Atwood's blog, quoted as the source recounting the duck anecdote.

- **Interplay Entertainment** — video game company; the animated chess game (Battle Chess) is the origin of the "duck" story.

- **Disney** — its 1940s animators originated the "hairy arms" tactic.

- **Tina Fey** — *inferred reference*. The improv "say yes" philosophy and the lines about innovation happening "in places where 'No' is seldom heard" echo her book *Bossypants*. Flagged as an inference; the highlight does not name her explicitly.
