# [2] Stakeholders Are People Too — Articulating Design Decisions

Source: [[Articulating Design Decisions - Tom Greever]]

> [!Summary]
> Stakeholders are not an obstacle to design but individuals with distinct values, pressures, and ways of seeing the work. To communicate effectively, you have to understand what each role cares about. Executives need to be brought up to speed quickly and want to give feedback efficiently. Developers and engineers are analytical, juggling bug backlogs and roadmaps, and often don't even notice the UI details you labored over. Product owners, project managers, and the marketing/content/creative functions each bring their own priorities. Knowing these differences lets you tailor how you present and defend decisions to the person in front of you.

**Highlights**

### Stakeholder Values

- Everyone is unique, and you must consider them in different ways no matter their role.

### Executives or Managers

- Your job is to bring them up to speed as quickly as possible, present your solutions, and solicit their feedback.

### Developers or Engineers

- Developers are usually looking at a backlog of bugs and enhancements on the one hand, and a roadmap for future development that's bigger than they can manage on the other. They tend to be more analytical, so they don't even "see" some of the UI details you've designed.
